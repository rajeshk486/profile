# The Employee Lifecycle: A Director-Level Interview Framework

**The strategic frame:** Most managers describe hiring, onboarding, and reviews as isolated tasks. A Director-level answer treats them as one continuous system with feedback loops — a headcount decision in Phase 1 determines your retention risk in Phase 6, and how you handle Phase 7 shapes your employer brand for Phase 1 of the next cycle. When you answer, name the phase, name the system-level lever you pulled, and tie it to a business metric (attrition %, time-to-productivity, backfill cost).

---

## Phase 1 — Workforce Planning & Requisition
*"Do we actually need to hire, and for what?"*

| | |
|---|---|
| **What it demonstrates** | You think in capacity models and org design, not headcount requests |
| **Key artifacts** | Skills matrix, capacity vs. roadmap model, build-vs-borrow-vs-buy analysis, leveling/ratio guidelines (e.g. your 2 SDE2 : 7 SDE1 mix) |
| **Common failure mode interviewers probe for** | Reactive hiring — backfilling a role without questioning if the role itself is still right |

**Interview questions**
- "Walk me through how you decided you needed to hire, rather than restructure or upskill the existing team."
- "How do you determine the right seniority mix for a team — why 2 senior : 7 junior, not the reverse?"
- "Tell me about a time you *pushed back* on a headcount request from your own leadership."
- "How do you forecast hiring needs 2 quarters ahead when the roadmap keeps shifting?"

---

## Phase 2 — Sourcing & Recruitment
*"How do we get the right candidates into the funnel?"*

| | |
|---|---|
| **What it demonstrates** | Employer branding instinct, funnel-metric literacy, partnership with TA |
| **Key artifacts** | Job description quality, sourcing channel mix, referral program design, funnel conversion rates |
| **Common failure mode interviewers probe for** | Treating recruiting as HR's job entirely, no involvement until final interview |

**Interview questions**
- "What's your role in sourcing versus your recruiter's? Where's the line?"
- "How have you used your engineering brand (talks, writing, open source) to pull inbound candidates?"
- "Describe a time a role sat open too long — what did you diagnose and change?"
- "How do you widen a candidate pool without diluting the bar?"

---

## Phase 3 — Selection & Hiring
*"How do we evaluate and decide, fast and fairly?"*

| | |
|---|---|
| **What it demonstrates** | Structured decision-making, bias mitigation, ability to sell candidates on joining |
| **Key artifacts** | Interview scorecards/rubrics, panel design, calibration process, offer-close conversion rate |
| **Common failure mode interviewers probe for** | Gut-feel hiring, inconsistent bar across interviewers, losing candidates at offer stage |

**Interview questions**
- "Tell me about a hiring decision where the panel disagreed. How did you resolve it?"
- "How do you keep interview bar consistent across 9 different interviewers?"
- "Describe a hire you almost made but didn't — what was the signal?"
- "How do you close a strong candidate who has a competing offer?"

---

## Phase 4 — Onboarding
*"How fast do they become productive, and do they stay?"*

| | |
|---|---|
| **What it demonstrates** | You engineer time-to-first-commit / time-to-productivity, not just "welcome kits" |
| **Key artifacts** | 30-60-90 plans, buddy/mentor system, onboarding docs, first-project design |
| **Common failure mode interviewers probe for** | No measurable ramp plan; new hires "figure it out" |

**Interview questions**
- "How do you structure the first 30 days for a new SDE1 versus a lateral SDE2 hire?"
- "What's your time-to-first-meaningful-contribution metric, and how do you shorten it?"
- "Tell me about an onboarding failure — someone who didn't ramp — and what you changed afterward."
- "How do you onboard someone into a legacy system (e.g. mid-migration, like a C#-to-Java move) without overwhelming them?"

---

## Phase 5 — Development, Performance & Growth
*"How do they grow, and how do we know they're performing?"*

| | |
|---|---|
| **What it demonstrates** | Career-pathing ability, calibrated performance management, coaching over commanding |
| **Key artifacts** | Growth/promo frameworks, 1:1 cadence, skip-levels, performance calibration, PIP process |
| **Common failure mode interviewers probe for** | Annual-review-only feedback, no individualized growth plans, avoiding hard conversations |

**Interview questions**
- "How do you build a growth path for an SDE1 who wants to become a tech lead in 18 months?"
- "Tell me about managing a low performer — what was your process before it became a PIP?"
- "How do you calibrate performance ratings fairly across 9 reports with different scopes?"
- "Describe a time you gave feedback that was hard to deliver. How did you frame it?"
- "How do you keep a high performer challenged when there's no title change available?"

---

## Phase 6 — Retention & Engagement
*"How do we keep the people we don't want to lose?"*

| | |
|---|---|
| **What it demonstrates** | Proactive attrition-risk management, not reactive counter-offers |
| **Key artifacts** | Stay interviews, engagement survey action plans, compensation-benchmarking cadence, flight-risk tracking |
| **Common failure mode interviewers probe for** | Only noticing disengagement at resignation; no systematic pulse-check |

**Interview questions**
- "How do you identify flight risk before someone starts interviewing elsewhere?"
- "Tell me about a time you retained someone who was about to leave — what actually worked?"
- "How do you keep a team engaged through something unglamorous, like a multi-quarter legacy migration?"
- "What's your philosophy on counter-offers?"
- "How do you measure team health beyond attrition rate?"

---

## Phase 7 — Offboarding, Transition & Alumni
*"How do we handle the exit — and the relationship after it?"*

| | |
|---|---|
| **What it demonstrates** | You protect knowledge continuity and employer brand even on the way out |
| **Key artifacts** | Knowledge-transfer checklists, exit interview synthesis (fed back into Phase 1/6), alumni network |
| **Common failure mode interviewers probe for** | Treating exits as pure loss/admin, no knowledge capture, burning bridges |

**Interview questions**
- "Walk me through your offboarding process when a key contributor resigns."
- "How do you prevent single points of failure (bus factor) on a 9-person team?"
- "Tell me about an exit interview insight that changed how you managed the team afterward."
- "How do you handle team morale when someone well-liked leaves?"
- "Would any of your former reports come back to work for you? Why?"

---

## How to use this in the room

1. **Anchor every answer to a phase name** — "That's really a Phase 5 problem, growth pathing" signals systems thinking before you even give the example.
2. **Close the loop** — the strongest answers show data flowing backward: exit-interview insight (Phase 7) → changed onboarding (Phase 4) → improved retention (Phase 6). That loop *is* the Director-level signal.
3. **Have one story per phase ready**, ideally drawn from your own team of 9 — a real STAR example beats a framework recitation every time.
4. **Quantify wherever possible** — time-to-productivity, attrition %, offer-acceptance rate, backfill cost. Directors are evaluated on whether they *measure* the system, not just run it.
